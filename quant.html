Tier methodology

Every company in the universe sits in one of four tiers: Anchor, Conviction, Tail or Watch. The tier reflects three things: whether the company is compounding value, whether its shares can be bought and sold at size, and how strong investor demand is. Scores on these three pillars suggest a tier; the investment team confirms it, and any departure from the suggested tier is recorded with its reason.

Anchor

Proven value creation, deep liquidity, strong demand. Category leaders, typically valued at $50bn or more, quoted across several secondary venues and with a record of rising valuations.

Portfolio role: core holdings that provide stability and the most reliable path to liquidity.

Conviction

Strong value accretion with growing liquidity. Companies compounding value quickly where secondary markets are still developing, or established names where one pillar lags.

Portfolio role: higher-upside positions backed by a specific investment thesis.

Tail

Scored, but weak or thinly traded. Companies with limited secondary liquidity, little evidence of value accretion, or a risk that caps their tier.

Portfolio role: smaller positions that add optionality, monitored for upgrade.

Watch

On our radar, not yet assessable. Companies without enough secondary pricing or funding data to score. They move to a scored tier as soon as the data allows.

Portfolio role: monitored, not held.

The three pillars

Each pillar is scored 0–100 from the measures below. A measure with no data is left out and the remaining measures are re-weighted, so missing data neither helps nor hurts.

Value accretion

40%

Is the company compounding value?

  • Secondary price vs last valuation (40%): −30% scores 0, +60% scores 100. Only marks from the last 18 months count.
  • Valuation growth (30%): annual growth in valuation across priced rounds over three years or more, or since the first priced round if that is at least a year earlier; 0% scores 0, 150% a year scores 100.
  • Freshness of the last valuation (15%): a mark within 3 months scores 100, 36 months or older scores 0.
  • Path to realisation (15%): an IPO filing or a company-run tender in the last 24 months scores 100.

Liquidity

30%

Can positions be entered and exited at size?

  • Venue coverage (30%): number of venues quoting the company, out of Clarity, Augment and Forge.
  • Price agreement (25%): gap between the highest and lowest venue price; 30% scores 0, 3% scores 100. A single venue scores 40.
  • Scale (30%): valuation on a log scale; $1bn scores 0, $300bn scores 100.
  • Company liquidity programmes (15%): a company-run tender or buyback in the last 24 months scores 100.

Companies with a researched funding history are scored on liquidity even if no venue quotes them; the absence of quotes counts as zero coverage.

Demand

30%

How strong is buyer interest?

  • Setter 30 ranking (40%): rank on the secondary market's most-demanded list; #1 scores 100, unranked scores 0.
  • Clarity 50 ranking (30%): rank on Clarity's most-traded list; #1 scores 100, unranked scores 0.
  • Price momentum (30%): change since our first verified secondary price; −20% scores 0, +30% scores 100.

From scores to tiers

The composite score weights the pillars 40 / 30 / 30. The suggested tier follows these rules, applied in order:

Suggested tierRule
WatchFewer than two pillars are backed by data. The company is monitored, not held, until it can be scored. Companies the investment team has placed in Anchor or Conviction keep that tier.
AnchorComposite score of 60 or more, liquidity score of 50 or more, and valuation of $50bn or more.
TailLiquidity score below 30, whatever the other pillars show.
ConvictionComposite score of 40 or more, or a value accretion score of 70 or more.
TailEverything else.

Risk overlay

The overlay can only lower a tier, never raise it. It captures risks the three pillars cannot measure:

  • Regulatory: the business model's legal standing is unsettled, for example prediction markets or digital assets.
  • Geopolitical and structural: export controls, sanctions exposure, or offshore holding structures.
  • Binary or technical: value depends on an unproven technology milestone or a single outcome.
  • Transfer restrictions: rights of first refusal or company consent that make positions hard to build or exit.

Governance and review

  • Investment team decides: scores suggest a tier; the team confirms it. Every difference between the suggested and confirmed tier is recorded with its reason and date.
  • Quarterly review of all tiers, plus an immediate review when a company raises a round, runs a tender, files for an IPO, or an overlay risk changes.
  • Data-limited companies sit on Watch unless the team has assigned them a tier, and are re-scored as secondary pricing and funding history are added.

Methodology & sources

  • Secondary prices are indicative per-share prices from Clarity (formerly Hiive) company pages, Augment company pages (data by Caplight) and Forge public company pages. Indicative prices are not executed trades.
  • Implied valuation = average available venue price × shares outstanding. Share counts are Perennial estimates; for companies outside the Top 50 tracker they are derived from Clarity's implied valuation.
  • Premium / discount compares implied valuation with the last primary round's post-money valuation. Where that round is more than 18 months old and the company has since run a tender offer or buyback, the latest company-run tender valuation is used instead (marked "tender"); investor-to-investor sales are never used. By default only rounds dated within 18 months are used for summary statistics; older or undated rounds are shown faded.
  • Private companies only: companies that have listed on a public exchange are removed from the universe (checked 12 Oct 2026). Prices quoted in currencies other than USD (Mistral AI, Monzo) are excluded from cross-venue statistics.
  • Liquidity score is Perennial's composite of observed secondary pricing (45 pts), trading attention on venue rankings (35 pts) and company scale (20 pts).
  • All figures in USD. Universe from Perennial's Pre-IPO Fund Universe; prices refreshed .
